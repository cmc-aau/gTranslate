Validation

The results below are from the gTranslate manuscript (Chaumeil et al., 2026), where the full results are available. They apply to the models distributed with gTranslate 0.0.4 and later. Models were trained on 356,020 genomes from GTDB R09-RS220 (see Models), and the expected translation table of each genome was established from its taxonomic classification. gTranslate was compared to the coding density heuristic used by CheckM, Codetta (Shulgina & Eddy, 2021), and the translation table information provided by NCBI.

Cross-validation

Using 5-fold cross-validation across the 356,020 GTDB R09-RS220 genomes, the best individual classifier (k-nearest neighbours) made 15 misclassifications and the best ensembles 14. By comparison, the CheckM heuristic made 101 misclassifications, and Codetta made 444 or 78 when requiring 8 or 34 alignment columns, respectively, to support a UGA reassignment. NCBI translation table information disagreed with the expected translation table for 62 of the 207,495 genomes for which it was available.

Taxonomically novel genomes

To assess performance on genomes from taxa absent from the training data, all genomes from a single species, genus or family were removed, the classifiers were trained on the remaining genomes, and the removed genomes were classified. Performance decreased with taxonomic novelty, and ensembles of classifiers performed more consistently than individual classifiers. The ensemble used by gTranslate (AdaBoost, decision tree, k-nearest neighbours, multi-layer perceptron and XGBoost) was selected as it had the fewest misclassifications across the cross-validation and leave-one-taxon-out tests.

These test sets include genomes from many taxa absent from the training data, and the GlobDB genomes come from metagenomic studies not represented in the GTDB.

Most disagreements appear to be errors in the expected translation table rather than in the predictions. For example, five Ca. Stammera capleta genomes predicted to use table 11 form a basal lineage of this species with genomic evidence of using table 11, and 22 Minisyncoccia genomes predicted to use table 4 have genomic evidence of a UGA reassignment to tryptophan. After accounting for such cases, gTranslate made 16 misclassifications across the 250,583 test genomes (99.994% accuracy), compared to 25 for the CheckM heuristic and 34 for Codetta. Twelve of the 16 misclassifications were between tables 4 and 25, giving an accuracy of 98.9% across the 1,077 genomes not using table 11. Unlike the CheckM heuristic, gTranslate distinguishes between tables 4 and 25.

Computational performance

gTranslate requires approximately 20 seconds per genome per CPU, approximately 100 times less than Codetta.
